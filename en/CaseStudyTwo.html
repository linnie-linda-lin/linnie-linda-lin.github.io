THE PROJECT

HealthHACK is Intelligent Helse's annual hackathon where students from different disciplines come together to solve real health challenges. Our brief came from Rælingen municipality.

Demand for municipal health and care services is growing while available resources are limited. The goal is for as many people as possible to manage independently without relying on direct support from the municipality.

One group that often falls through the cracks are young adults. Many of them struggle to manage everyday life and find it difficult to live alone and experience social exclusion.

Our target group with traits:

Young adults aged 18-30

ADHD

Autism spectrum diagnoses

Anxiety

RESEARCH

We started by speaking directly with a representative from Rælingen municipality to understand the problem space and the people affected. This gave us insight into the gap between what the municipality can provide and what these young adults actually need day-to-day. Rælingen municipality estimates around 50 young adults in the municipality alone are in a similar situation.

PROCESS

With the brief and conversation with the Rælingen municipality, we developed a persona:

  • Tom, a 19-year-old with ADHD moving out for the first time.
  • He has never lived alone, lacks organisational skills and has no family support.
  • Over time, laundry piles up, groceries rot in the fridge and his mental health begins to decline.

This led us to explore: how might we reduce the number of young adults dependent on municipal support? There was no predefined problem or solution as we had to narrow it down ourselves.

Through Tom, we explored the reasons why young adults move out, what conditions make them vulnerable, who is most at risk of falling through the cracks and what happens when they do.

One thing became clear:

How we take care of our home often reflects how we are doing mentally and socially.

Most people learn these skills from family and close ones, but not everyone has access to that kind of support.

This is where we saw the opportunity. And it led us to our core problem statement:

How can we support neurodivergent young adults in building habits around essential household tasks?

THE SOLUTION

Klar for livet builds on existing platforms already used by Rælingen municipality. Our solution offers cognitively friendly, accessible videos and tools for daily household tasks such as groceries, cleaning and saving money.

The concept includes planned features such as personalised reminders and a Welcome to Young-Adult Life package with QR codes linking to instructional videos.

Developed in 48 hours, Klar for livet is just the beginning. Our roadmap outlines the next steps to bring it to life from validating the concept with real users to back-end development and a full pilot with Rælingen municipality.

PITCH & FEEDBACK

We pitched Klar for livet to a panel of judges, competing against teams from across OsloMet.

The judges responded positively to building on platforms Rælingen municipality already uses, rather than proposing an expensive new system. They highlighted the concept's practicality and scalability, and appreciated that it was grounded in real user needs rather than technology for its own sake.

REFLECTION

HealthHACK 2026 was my third hackathon, and the first one where I won. From earlier contests, I learned how important storytelling is, backed up by research, clear reasoning and a human-centred perspective.

Working within a limited timeframe pushed me to make decisions fast and trust my own process, as well as take charge when the team needed direction. As the sole interaction designer on the team, I took on a broad range of responsibilities. I took the lead on redesigning the interface for Rælingen municipality's website and Instagram prototype, editing the reel video, building the PowerPoint presentation and delivering the pitch.

The most challenging and valuable lesson was learning how to narrow an open brief quickly and purposefully. If we had had more time, I would have loved to speak directly with neurodivergent young adults, conduct more interviews with counsellors and run proper user testing to validate the concept before pitching it.
