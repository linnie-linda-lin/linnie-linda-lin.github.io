THE PROJECT

Tieto Banktech gave us an open brief: building a future AI banking agent. With significant freedom to define the problem space ourselves, no predefined user problem and no fixed solution, we had four requirements to meet:

Clear AI labelling

Traceability

Secure data handling

No financial advice

RESEARCH

I used a mixed-method approach to understand how people use digital banking today and where AI could provide real value.

Survey

An interesting early finding was that it wasn't simply about being for or against AI. It depended on what we were asking AI to do. Comfort generally decreases as AI moves from supporting the user towards acting on their behalf. This led us to explore where users draw the line between AI supporting them and AI acting on their behalf.

In-depth interviews

While the survey gave us broader patterns, the interviews gave us the reasoning behind them. Transcripts were analysed through open coding, then brought together through affinity mapping.

From synthesis, five recurring themes emerged: building trust in AI, human oversight, understanding financial information, proactive assistance and inclusive design.

Narrowing these themes down, three key insights emerged:

Understand finances

Reduce effort

Maintain control

Users want to quickly understand what has happened in their finances. People regularly check their bank to look for changes in their balance and transactions, but they still have to spend time interpreting this information and what those changes actually mean to them. In short:

Key insight

Users want help that is simple, clear and trustworthy while still remaining in control.

PROCESS

Ideation

The research raised two central questions that shaped the direction of the project:

  • How should AI fit into the banking experience?
  • How can AI uniquely add value?

To explore possible answers, I facilitated a Crazy 4s workshop with the team to generate a broad range of ideas across trust, financial clarity, human support and AI interaction. We then developed five How Might We questions to bridge the research insights and ideation.

The hardest part of this project wasn't designing the solution, but narrowing the problem space. As the sole UX designer on the team, I was responsible for driving this narrowing process and defining the design opportunity:

Users want to quickly understand their finances. They want to be informed but not overwhelmed. They want to be in control, but they also want support. Traditional banking already shows balances, graphs and tables, but this information is static, and it is still the users who have to interpret what it means for their financial situation. Therefore, understanding and sense-making while connecting, comparing and explaining financial information was where AI could genuinely reduce cognitive effort in a way traditional banking interfaces cannot.

Problem statement

How might we use AI to reduce the cognitive effort required to understand personal financial information while keeping the user informed and in control?

User testing

The concept was first developed as a mid-fidelity prototype which I tested remotely with four participants from the earlier interviews. Each entered Verdt å vite through an illustrative version of their own bank's app and could freely follow links to the underlying transactions. Since participants used the mobile prototype on a laptop, the testing focused on the concept and the role of AI rather than mobile navigation.

→ View the user testing prototype in Figma

Four key findings:

  • Users want to check the AI's work.
    Participants repeatedly opened the transactions behind an explanation, and one said they felt safer because they could fact-check the AI. In the high-fidelity version, I placed the summary first with the sources directly below, so users get the conclusion and can verify it right after.
  • It wasn't clear what the AI actually did.
    Participants were unsure which parts came from AI and which from the bank. I added clearer AI labelling and the "Slik fungerer AI-forklaringen" entry point: the bank provides the facts, and AI connects and explains them.
  • An observation can easily become advice.
    Participants understood that the AI explains rather than decides, but the music subscription insight still hinted that users could save money by dropping one service. The bank sees payments, not why people chose them, so the line between comparison and suggestion is something I'd keep refining.
  • Risks I hadn't anticipated.
    One participant pointed out that in relationships involving financial control, an automatic overview could make it easier to monitor someone's spending. Sensitive purchases, such as medical bills, could also feel uncomfortable to see resurfaced. Users should be able to exclude certain purchases and turn Verdt å vite off.

With four participants, these findings are directional rather than proof, but they shaped the final high-fidelity design.

THE SOLUTION

Verdt å vite

Verdt å vite is a proactive AI understanding layer integrated into banking. Instead of requiring users to ask questions, it identifies meaningful changes and patterns in existing financial data and explains what happened in simple language. AI does the summarising; the user does the understanding.

REFLECTION

This project taught me how to work in a genuinely open-ended design process. The hardest challenge wasn't designing the solution, but defining the right problem.

The most valuable lesson was learning to question whether AI is actually necessary before designing with it. In this project, AI earned its place because it genuinely reduced cognitive effort for real people. Being critical of its purpose and always asking why is something I will carry into every project going forward.

I also got to explore beyond pure UX/UI, into marketing and business considerations, which gave me a broader perspective on how design decisions connect to real-world impact.
